Mark Dean

Mark Dean is a computer engineer who helped shape the personal computer revolution. He was chief engineer of the 12-person team that designed the original IBM PC in the early 1980s, and he holds three of the nine original patents for that machine. He also co-developed the Industry Standard Architecture (ISA) bus, which made it possible to plug devices like printers and monitors directly into a computer.

He grew up in Jefferson City, Tennessee, where he was both a star athlete and a straight A student. In sixth grade, his white friend asked if he was really Black, because he was “too smart to be Black.” That moment stuck with him. He later said: “There may be obstacles, but there are no limits.”

His career at IBM spanned over 30 years. In 1995, he became the first African American to be named an IBM Fellow, the company’s highest technical honor. In 1997, he was inducted into the National Inventors Hall of Fame. He later led the team that created the first 1 GHz microprocessor. After retiring from IBM in 2013, he returned to his alma mater the University of Tennessee as a distinguished professor.
